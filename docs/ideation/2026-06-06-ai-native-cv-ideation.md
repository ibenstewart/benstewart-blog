# Ideation: AI-native /cv for benstewart.ai — interrogating the spec

> Generated by `ce-ideate`. Six independent ideation frames were run over the existing `/cv` build spec plus fresh `last30days` research on how people are putting "AI thinking" onto personal sites. The spec was treated as a baseline to interrogate, not a thing to refine.

## Headline finding

Six independent frames (pain & friction, inversion/removal, assumption-breaking, leverage/compounding, cross-domain analogy, constraint-flipping) converged — without coordination — on the same four conclusions, which substantially rewrite the spec:

1. **The "hire-me CV" framing is wrong** for a sitting Senior Director on a thought-leadership platform.
2. **The three reader-selected lenses** are the most try-hard, lowest-payoff mechanic in the spec.
3. **The bespoke CV chatbot duplicates the planned "Ask the archive" (todo #9)** and is, per the research, commoditised and weakly-converting — merge it or don't ship it in v1.
4. **The boring machine-readable spine** is the genuinely high-signal, low-risk win the spec buries in Phase 3.

Convergence at this strength is a strong signal the survivors are real, not artefacts of a single lens.

**The spec broke its own cardinal rule.** It calls Ben "VP of Engineering" (the live /bio says Senior Director), "20+ years leading" (~14, since 2012), and asserts a "BSc Internet Software Development, Glasgow Caledonian" that /bio does not list. Fact-drift inside a single spec doc is the strongest argument for survivors 5 and 6.

## Grounding Context (codebase)

- Next.js 15 / App Router / Tailwind v4 / MDX blog. `app/speaking/page.tsx` is the clean data-array page template to copy for a `resume.json`-driven `/cv`.
- **Structured-data spine already exists:** `app/components/JsonLd.tsx` exports a flat `PersonJsonLd` (no stable `@id`, no `ProfilePage` wrapper); house convention escapes `<`→`<` in every JSON-LD block. Extend this spine, do not fork it.
- **Dependencies:** `postgres` v3.4.7 and `framer-motion` v12 installed (both largely unused); **no** Anthropic SDK, embeddings lib, or vector DB — the agent is greenfield. New routes must be hand-added to `app/sitemap.ts`.
- **Bio facts:** Senior Director (2024), Director (2020), Senior Eng Manager (2019, joined Skyscanner); CityFM 2012–~2018 (Team Lead → Head of SW Eng); earlier roles to 2006 (Arnold Clark). Leading since 2012 (~14 yrs). No degree listed. Wife, daughter, dog; Glasgow; golf beginner.
- **Roadmap overlap (todo.md):** #9 "Ask the archive" (RAG chat over the blog), #4 Field Reports (the blog's spine series), #6 embeddings-based related-posts + TLDRs, #11 predictions tracker, #12 "things I changed my mind about", #2 newsletter, #5 GitHub repo (just stood up).
- The blog is explicitly a **thought-leadership platform** and Ben is a *sitting* Senior Director — the central tension a "hire-me CV" framing has to resolve.

## Topic Axes

1. Framing & audience — what the artefact fundamentally is and who it's for
2. Page craft & joy — the static reading experience
3. The conversational agent — the "ask" layer
4. Credibility, honesty & showing-the-work — the trust layer
5. Machine-readability & distribution — structured spine, PDF, findability

## Ranked Ideas

### 1. Reframe it as a reflection on how he leads, not a "hire-me" CV
**Description:** Every frame hit the same tension: Ben is a *sitting* Senior Director running a thought-leadership blog, so a pitch-y CV is the single most try-hard thing on the site. Resolve it by making the artefact a standing "how I work / what would actually move me / who I'm a genuine fit for — and who I'm not" reflection. A recruiter still gets everything; a peer gets judgment, not availability. Subsumes the "bouncer", "/working page", "dating-profile fit/anti-fit", and "still in the chair" variants.
**Axis:** Framing & audience
**Basis:** `direct:` grounding — "Ben is a *sitting* Senior Director — NOT obviously job-hunting. This creates real tension with a 'hire me' CV framing... one of the richest reframe angles"; `external:` the CV-as-reflection trend ("shows judgment, not inventory"). Reached independently by all 6 frames.
**Rationale:** This is the load-bearing decision — PDF? agent? lenses? all cascade from whether this is an application or a leadership artefact. Reframing kills the try-hard read the skeptical research says destroys trust, and fits the platform.
**Downsides:** "How I work" can drift into vague navel-gazing without the CV's factual spine; an anti-fit list is read by his current employer too; forgoes the clean utility of a standard CV for the rare real recruiter.
**Confidence:** 88%
**Complexity:** Low (a positioning + copy decision, not code)
**Status:** Unexplored

### 2. Lead with the Field Report; make the page the exhibit it links to
**Description:** Invert the spec's ordering (the field report is Phase 4, last). The post — "I was going to build an AI-native CV; here's what I actually built and why most of the genre is try-hard" — is the asset that travels (LinkedIn #10, newsletter), it's on-brand (#4 Field Reports = the blog's spine), and the spec itself calls it "the actual signal for both job tracks." Writing-first de-risks everything: ship a real artefact in one session, then let what the writing surfaces decide how much page/agent to build.
**Axis:** Credibility, honesty & showing-the-work
**Basis:** `direct:` spec Phase 4 ("the actual signal") + todo #4 (Field Reports spine); `external:` Nielsen ("what it reveals about your judgment is the story"), the build-in-public meta-pattern ("static CVs are anti-signal").
**Rationale:** Ben's credibility comes from published thinking, not a RAG bot. Leading with the report resolves the job-hunt-vs-thought-leadership tension by choosing thought-leadership outright, and produces the highest-travel artefact first.
**Downsides:** A post with no live artefact under it is just talk; risks perpetual "I'll build the page later"; the writing is the expensive part (4–6 hrs).
**Confidence:** 82%
**Complexity:** Low–Medium
**Status:** Unexplored

### 3. Cut the three lenses; ship one honest page
**Description:** Three frames independently said delete the lens selector. It forces the reader to self-classify into a hiring funnel (two of three lenses assume job-hunting, which mostly doesn't apply), it's the most screenshot-mockable "look at my interactive CV" element, and it's per-bullet tagging overhead forever. Replace with one linear read in one voice; if depth control is wanted, key it off "short vs long", not imagined hiring intent. Sharper optional variant: a curated "tasting-menu" sequence where Ben shows *why* each item is in this order — judgment over configuration.
**Axis:** Page craft & joy
**Basis:** `direct:` spec's three lenses + grounding framing tension; `external:` Nielsen (an over-engineered surface that hides process is what FAILS; "decoration without depth reads as marketing").
**Rationale:** The lens picker is interaction cost with no payoff if the reader isn't a recruiter slotting Ben into a req, and it leaks the job-hunt premise on first contact. One honest page is less to build, less to maintain, harder to mock, and looks like it belongs to the (toggle-free) blog.
**Downsides:** Lenses were the spec's headline "joy"; cutting risks a flatter page; loses the real value of letting a busy recruiter jump to their angle.
**Confidence:** 78%
**Complexity:** Low (removal)
**Status:** Unexplored

### 4. One grounded bot, two doors — merge with "Ask the archive", or ship no bot in v1
**Description:** The spec's CV chatbot overlaps todo #9 "Ask the archive" almost entirely (same corpus, guardrails, eval burden) and the repo has zero AI infra today. Build ONE grounded service over posts + a resume identity block, exposed on `/cv` and the blog, sharing one eval suite + rate-limiter. Given weak conversion and commoditisation in the research, seriously weigh shipping **NO bot in v1** — the only prized behaviour (honest abstention) can ship as static copy ("here's what I won't pretend to know — email me") for ~£0. If a bot does ship, point it at Ben: log the questions, digest them weekly, and mine them for Field Reports (the n8n inversion — the bot is recon for Ben, not impression-management for them).
**Axis:** The conversational agent
**Basis:** `direct:` grounding — "#9 massively overlaps the spec's `/cv` agent... MERGE... a live, high-value question"; the agent is greenfield; `external:` about.me AI-Twin commoditised, HN "novelty gone", CNBC weak conversion (2 interviews/140 apps; ~30/700), n8n conversation-log-as-signal.
**Rationale:** One bot audited once and deployed twice halves the most expensive, highest-risk work in both spec and roadmap, and the "signal-for-Ben" framing sidesteps the entire "does this even get me hired" doubt.
**Downsides:** Merging couples two roadmap timelines; a shared bot must serve two intents without blurring voice; "no bot" forgoes the biggest craft signal; logging raises a real privacy line to draw.
**Confidence:** 80%
**Complexity:** Medium (one bot) / Low (no bot)
**Status:** Unexplored

### 5. Make honesty structured and on-the-record — and let the spec's own title-drift be the worked example
**Description:** The honesty section is the spec's stated centrepiece, but the multiverse story is a vindication tale (a humblebrag), and the spec itself inflated Ben's title ("VP" vs real Senior Director), tenure ("20+" vs ~14 yrs leading), and an unverified degree. Make that the content: a short, dated, *structured* "what this isn't telling you / what I got wrong / what I'll predict" record that renders on the page, feeds the agent's gap-bait answers from honest source material, and shares DNA + infra with the predictions tracker (#11) and "things I changed my mind about" (#12). Optional sharp edges: publish the agent's eval scoreboard ("passed 47/50, here are the 3 it failed"); seed the agent with adversarial "cross-examination" prompts so it volunteers its own weak points.
**Axis:** Credibility, honesty & showing-the-work
**Basis:** `direct:` grounding key facts (title/tenure drift; unverified degree) + spec honesty section + todo #11/#12; `external:` Nielsen ("show the rejected drafts + the judgment layer"), Capterra (41% of jobseekers misrepresent with AI → any AI surface is suspect until proven honest), README-as-brand.
**Rationale:** Structuring honesty (not just writing it) is the only thing that makes "facts stay true" a property of the system rather than a chore — and it's unfakeable, exactly Ben's register, and reusable across three roadmap items.
**Downsides:** How raw is a genuine values call (current employer reads it too); a failure-led page could read as career-risky for a sitting director; structured > prose is more work.
**Confidence:** 85%
**Complexity:** Low–Medium
**Status:** Unexplored

### 6. Spine first: ProfilePage + a stable @id, generated from one resume.json, works in `curl`
**Description:** The genuinely high-signal, low-risk, low-effort move the spec buries in Phase 3, and five frames converged on it. Upgrade the existing flat `PersonJsonLd` to `ProfilePage` with nested `Person`; give it a stable `@id` reused verbatim on `/bio`, `/cv`, and the homepage (this is what merges into a Google Knowledge Panel); add `sameAs` / `knowsAbout` / `alumniOf`. Generate every surface — page, JSON-LD, `.md` mirror, and the PDF *if* it ships — from one `resume.json`, so a fact can never drift across six places (the failure the spec already committed). Server-render so `curl benstewart.ai/cv` returns the whole CV; the real non-human readers (ATS, Google, IDE agents) don't run JS. Reuse the repo's `<` escaping convention.
**Axis:** Machine-readability & distribution
**Basis:** `direct:` grounding implementation prior art ("use ProfilePage with nested Person; a stable @id... merges into a Google Knowledge Panel... low-effort, high-signal regardless of the chatbot") + repo reality (`JsonLd.tsx` spine exists — "extend, don't fork"; escaping convention) + learnings (manual sitemap add).
**Rationale:** It can't hallucinate, can't be jailbroken, pays off every time anyone Googles him, and lays the canonical-identity foundation the agent later cites. Highest signal-per-hour on the board.
**Downsides:** It's the un-sexy part, easy to skip for the flashy agent; a Knowledge Panel is not guaranteed; "one source generates all" has upfront plumbing cost.
**Confidence:** 90%
**Complexity:** Low–Medium
**Status:** Unexplored

## The thread that ties 1, 2 and 5 together

The most defensible version of this whole thing might be **less a `/cv` and more "a senior leader thinking in public about how to represent yourself in the AI era"** — which is exactly the blog's beat, and the one move no commoditised AI-twin can copy.

## Live tension to resolve (carried, not rejected)

The **boring PDF** splits the frames. One frame argued it's the *actual product* (recruiters search by skills first; ATS parsing is where most CVs die), and the others argued to cut it (a sitting Senior Director rarely feeds an ATS; "75% auto-rejection" is a myth). The resolution depends entirely on Survivor 1's answer: if this is not a job-application artefact, the PDF is optional and, per Survivor 6, falls out of `resume.json` for free if ever needed.
